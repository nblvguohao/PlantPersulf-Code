Abstract
Hydrogen sulfide (H2S) is a gaseous signaling molecule reported to play multiple roles in fruit ripening. However, the molecular 
mechanisms underlying H2S-mediated delay in fruit ripening remain to be established. Here, the gene encoding a WRKY tran-
scription factor, WRKY71, was 
identified as substantially upregulated in H2S-treated tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) via tran-
scriptome profiling. The expression of WRKY71 was negatively associated with that of CYANOALANINE SYNTHASE1 (CAS1). 
Transient 
and stable genetic modification experiments disclosed that WRKY71 acts as a repressor of the tomato ripening pro-
cess. CAS1 appears to play an opposite role, based on the finding that the ripening process was delayed in the cas1 mutant and 
accelerated in CAS1-OE tomatoes. 
Dual-luciferase reporter assay, yeast one-hybrid, electrophoretic mobility shift assay, and 
transient transformation experiments showed that WRKY71 bound to the CAS1 promoter and suppressed its activation. 
Moreover, the persulfidation of WRKY71 enhanced its binding ability to the CAS1 promoter. Data from luciferase complemen-
tation and Y2H assays confirmed that WRKY71 interacts with a BOI-related E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase 3 (BRG3) and is ubi -
quitinated in vitro. Further 
experiments showed that modification of BRG3 via persulfidation at Cys 206 and Cys212 led to 
reduced ubiquitination activity. Our findings support a model whereby BRG3 undergoes persulfidation at Cys 206 and 
Cys212, leading to reduced ubiquitination activity and decreased interactions with the WRKY71 transcript, with a subsequent 
increase in binding activity of the persulfidated WRKY71 to the CAS1 promoter, resulting in its transcriptional inhibition and 
thereby delayed ripening of tomatoes. Our collective findings provide insights into a mechanism of H2S-mediated regulation of 
tomato fruit ripening.

Introduction
Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) is a classical climacteric fruit 
extensively used as a model to study fruit ripening and sen -
escence. Multiple physiological and biochemical reactions 
occur during ripening and senescence of tomato fruits, in -
cluding pigment metabolism and transformation as well as 
synthesis and degradation of plant hormones ( Giovannoni 
et al. 2016), which are regulated by a series of transcription 
factors (TFs). WRKY, a unique TF family in plants, partici -
pates in plant signal transmission, ethylene biosynthesis, abi-
otic stress responses, and the process of ripening and 
senescence (Rushton et al. 2010). Generally, WRKY domains 
contain 1 or 2 highly conserved amino acid sequences 
(WRKYGQK) and a zinc-finger motif that binds the (T)(T) 
TGCA(C/T) element (W-box) of downstream gene promo
-
ters. In Arabidopsis ( Arabidopsis thaliana
), WRKY53 is re-
ported to regulate leaf senescence through binding the 
W-box and activating the expression of Senescence associated 
gene 12 (SAG12), CATALASE1/2/3 (
CAT1/2/3), and ORESARA 
9 (ORE9) (Schippers 2015). In tomatoes, WRKY35 enhances 
carotenoid accumulation by directly activating DXS1, 
WRKY31, 
and WRKY23 at the breaker and red-ripe maturity

stages (Bartley and Ishida 2003; Yuan et al. 2022 ). In peach 
(
Prunus persica), WRKY22 ubiquitination by ring-type E3 lig-
ase HOS1 is proposed to play a role in regulating the defense 
program (Li et al. 2020a, 2020b). A similar regulatory mode of 
WRKY45 has been reported, whereby its ubiquitination by 
nuclear ubiquitin-proteasome modulates the plant defense 
program in rice (Akane et al. 2013 ). The collective findings 
to date suggest that WRKYs are involved in various physio -
logical regulation mechanisms in plants through direct bind-
ing to target genes or posttranslational modifications. 
However, the issue of whether WRKY TFs can exert regula -
tory effects on tomato fruit ripening through ubiquitination 
remains unclear.
During fruit ripening, hydrogen cyanide (HCN), CO 2, and 
ethylene are concomitantly generated (Floss et al. 1965). 
Cyanoalanine synthase (CAS) is an enzyme that converts 
HCN to cyanoalanine and H
2S via the decyanidation pathway 
in plants (Warrilow 1998). García et al. (2014) reported in-
creased cyanogen content in roots, thereby inhibiting the 
formation of root hairs in plants with mitochondrial 
β
-cyanoalanine synthase CYS-C1 deletion in Arabidopsis. In 
avocado and carnation flowers, CAS plays a substantial role 
in decyanation. However, as CAS activity increases with the 
production of a large amount of ethylene, the HCN level is 
almost undetectable (
Manning 1986; Yip and Yang 1998). 
Recent reports have disclosed markedly higher CAS activity 
in climacteric than non-climacteric fruits (Mizutani et al. 
1988). In apples, CAS1 and CAS2 potentially play a role in 
cyanide detoxification during ripening (Sang et al. 2007). 
However, no direct evidence of a regulatory role of CAS in 
the ripening process in fruits has been obtained to date.
H2S is a gaseous signaling molecule with important physio-
logical functions in root formation, flower opening, stress re-
sponse, fruit ripening, and senescence (He et al. 2018; Zhang 
et al. 2020; Huang et al. 2021). Exogenous H2S suppresses res-
piration and ethylene production in bananas, strawberries, 
kiwi, and other fruits (
Hu et al. 2012; Li et al. 2017; Ge et al. 
2018). Upon knock-out of the endogenous H 2S production 
enzyme LCD1, the tomato fruit ripening process is acceler -
ated (Hu et al. 2020 ), indicating a regulatory role of H 2S in 
fruit ripening. However, the underlying molecular mechan -
isms remain to be established. Accumulating evidence has 
shown that ∼
5% proteins are persulfidated in Arabidopsis, 
a posttranslational modification whereby the thiol group at 
cysteine residues (–SH) is replaced with the persulfidation 
group (–SSH). The majority of persulfidated proteins are im-
plicated in the nuclear localization signal ( Khan et al. 2018), 
signal transduction (Chen et al. 2020), cell autophagy (Ma et 
al. 2021), and flowering processes (Laureano et al. 2020). Due 
to the different nucleophilic abilities of the –SH and –SSH 
groups, modification by persulfidation confers stronger 
chemical reactivity (Paul and Snyder 2012; Corpas 2019). 
For instance, persulfidation of Cys 160 of glyceraldehyde-3- 
phosphate dehydrogenase by H2S enhances its nuclear local-
ization (Khan et al. 2018). In guard cells, H2S-induced persul-
fidation of Cys 131 and Cys137 sites of OST1/SNF1-related 
protein kinase 2.6 (SnRK2.6) enhances kinase activity, there -
by promoting the closure of stomata ( Chen et al. 2020 ). 
Additionally, H2S is reported to inhibit the binding of FLC1 
and FLC3 to promoters of target genes through persulfida -
tion, thereby inducing the flowering process in Chinese cab -
bage (Ma et al. 2021). While H 2S-mediated persulfidation is 
clearly involved in several aspects of plant development, its 
precise role in the regulation of tomato fruit ripening is yet 
to be established.
To explore the mechanisms underlying H 2S regulation of 
the tomato ripening process, we screened the TF WRKY71 
responding to H 2S signal from transcriptome data of 
H2S-treated tomato fruit, which showed a negative associ -
ation with cyanoalanine synthase CAS1. Moreover, 
WRKY71 could interact with BRG3 and both proteins were 
modified by persulfidation. Based on the data obtained, we 
proposed that H 2S-mediated persulfidation of BRG3 and 
WRKY71 could weaken BRG3’s ubiquitination ability and en-
hance the binding ability of WRKY71 to CAS1
, thereby delay-
ing ripening of tomato fruit. To validate this hypothesis, we 
investigated the functions of WRKY71 and CAS1 in tomato 
fruit by transient expression and stable genetic system. The 
regulatory relationship between WRKY71 and CAS1 was 
identified by a dual-luciferase reporter, yeast one-hybrid 
(Y1H), electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA), and tran
-
sient transformation assays. Moreover, we identified persulfi-
dation of BRG3 weakened its ubiquitination ability and the 
interaction with WRKY71 by in vitro simulation experiments, 
yeast two-hybrid (Y2H), and luciferase complementation 
analysis. Data from the current study provide insights into 
the molecular mechanisms underlying H2S-mediated regula-
tion of tomato fruit ripening and posttranslational modifica-
tion of proteins.

WRKY71 inhibits target gene CAS1 expression to 
regulate tomato fruit ripening
A previous study showed that cyanoalanine synthase 1 
(CAS1) located downstream of the ethylene production 
pathway acts as a key rate-limiting enzyme in the decyanida-
tion pathway in plants. CAS1 and CAS2 function in cyanide 
detoxification in apples and are relevant in ethylene produc-
tion and conversion of HCN in avocado and carnation flow -
ers. Moreover, the activity of CAS is higher in climacteric 
compared with non-climacteric fruits ( Manning 1986; 
Mizutani et al. 1988; Warrilow 1988; Yip and Yang 1998; 
Sang et al. 2007). These findings suggest that CAS1 is a key 
gene involved in the fruit ripening process. In this study, 
CAS1 was screened via transcriptome profiling of 
H2S-treated tomato fruit (Fig. 1). Data obtained from a 
transient expression system, transgenic overexpression 
(CAS1-OE), and cas1 mutant plants support the role of 
CAS1 in promoting tomato ripening. Accordingly, CAS1 
was identified as a positive regulator of ripening (Fig. 5; 
Supplemental S4). Moreover, WRKY71 was shown to sup -
press CAS1 expression via binding the W-box ( Figs. 6, B
–D, 
and 7E). Based on the data, we propose that WRKY71 acts 
as a negative regulator of CAS1 transcription, thereby delay-
ing the tomato ripening process. To clarify the relationship 
between H2S and the tomato fruit ripening mechanism in -
volving the WRKY71-CAS1 axis, persulfidation detection 
was performed. The results disclosed no signal in CAS1 while 
WRKY71 was modified via persulfidation through the H 2S 
donor. Persulfidation increased the ability of WRKY71 to 
bind the W-box in the CAS1 promoter (Fig. 7). These findings 
were consistent with previous studies showing that persulfi -
dation increases enzyme activity and binding ability in 
Chinese cabbage ( Paul and Snyder 2012; Ma et al. 2021 ). 
Accordingly, we conclude that H 2S activates the transcrip -
tion of WRKY71 and induces the persulfidate posttransla -
tional modification of its protein to enhance its binding 
ability to the CAS1 promoter, thereby repressing CAS1 ex-
pression and causing a delay in tomato ripening.
Persulfidation of BRG3 at Cys206 and Cys212 decreases 
its ubiquitination activity and interactions with 
WRKY71
Protein ubiquitination is a common posttranslational modi-
fication that participates in the regulation of a wide variety of 
protein substrates in different cellular pathways through a 
3-enzyme cascade (E1–E2–E3) and identification of sub -
strates through Lys residues ( Goldknopf et al. 1977; Swatek 
and Komander 2016). For example, a ring-type E3 ligase, 
XB3, accelerates the ubiquitination and degradation of 
NAC2, ACS1, and ACO1 to delay ripening of bananas 
(Shan et al. 2020 ) and BRG2/3 ubiquitinates and degrades 
a negative regulator, MYB4, thereby accelerating ethylene ac-
cumulation to control banana fruit ripening ( Yang et al. 
2022). The results support a regulatory role of the ubiquitina-
tion process in the fruit ripening process. The potential role 
of persulfidation in ubiquitination may present a mechanism 
by which H2S mediates fruit ripening. In the current study, 
Y2H and luciferase complementation assays led to the iden -
tification of a ring-type E3 ligase, BRG3, that interacts with 
and ubiquitinates WRKY71 protein in vitro (
Fig. 8). It has 
been shown that BRG3 attenuates the inhibitory effect of 
WRKY71 on the fruit ripening process via degradation 
(Fig. 8
), inconsistent with results showing that H 2S delays 
the ripening tomato process by inducing an increase in 
WRKY71 transcription. Results obtained from LC-MS/MS 
and mutational analysis in the current study demonstrated 
that Cys
206 and Cys212 of BRG3 are modified via persulfida -
tion, with resulting weakening of ubiquitination ability 
(Fig. 9, B
–D). Cys206 and Cys212 were thus identified as critical 
residues for the ubiquitination ability of BRG3. Moreover, 
BRG3 presents an important target for H
2S-mediated regula-
tion of physiological processes in tomato fruits. Hence, it is 
reasonable to speculate that H2S signals decrease the ubiqui-
tination activity of BRG3 via persulfidation at Cys 206 and 
Cys212, thereby reducing the degradation of WRKY71. 
Theoretically, the factors underlying changes in protein func-
tion caused by persulfidation are not only the different nu -
cleophilic abilities of Cys-SSH but also changes in protein 
conformation. Further studies are essential to determine 
whether persulfidation causes changes in protein conform
-
ation and the mechanisms by which persulfidation of BRG3 
regulates tomato fruit growth and development in vivo
.
Conclusions
H2S is a gaseous signal molecule with multiple roles in fruit 
ripening and senescence. However, the molecular mechan -
isms underlying the H 2S-mediated delay in fruit ripening 
are currently unclear. In this study, we demonstrated that 
persulfidation of WRKY71, an H 2S-responsive ripening re -
pressor, increases the binding ability to the CAS1 promoter 
and suppresses the transcription of CAS1
, thereby delaying 
tomato ripening. Moreover, Cys206 and Cys212 of the E3 ubi-
quitin ligase, BRG3, were modified by persulfidation, which 
reduced its ubiquitination activity and interactions with 
WRKY71. Our collective findings support a model in which 
H2S persulfidates BRG3Cys206 and BRG3Cys212, resulting in re-
duced degradation of WRKY71. Meanwhile, persulfidation of 
WRKY71 promotes transcriptional inhibition of CAS1
, result-
ing in a delay in the ripening process of tomato fruit (Fig. 10).

The in vitro ubiquitination assay was performed according to 
the method of Abramovitch et al. (2006)with slight modifica-
tions. The ubiquitination reaction mixture (30 μ
l) comprising 
ubiquitination buffer (50 mM Tris HCl, pH 7.5, 2 mM ATP, 
5 mM MgCl 2, 30 mM phosphocreatine [Sigma-Aldrich, 
Saint Louis, USA]), 50 ng μl−1 creatine phosphokinase 
(Sigma-Aldrich), recombinant E1, E2, and ubiquitin (a small 
molecule protein) (Unibi Biotech, Changchun, Jilin, China) 
was incubated at 37 °C for 12 h. Proteins were separated using 
7.5% SDS-PAGE and identified via western blot with anti-Ub 
antibody (dilution is 1:5,000) and anti-GST antibody (dilution 
is 1:5,000) (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China).
