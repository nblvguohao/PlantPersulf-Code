Abstract 
Cysteine desulfhydrase catalyses the generation of the signaling molecule hydrogen sulfide (H2S) in plants. In this study, we found that 
H2S can inhibit tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) fruit ripening and SlWRKY6 undergoes differential protein persulfidation in SlLCD1- 
overexpressing leaves. Then, further study indicated that SlWRKY6 could be persulfidated by H 2S at Cys396. By construction of 
slwrky6 mutants and SlWRKY6-OE lines, we found that SlWRKY6 positively regulates leaf senescence and fruit ripening by activating 
the transcription of ripening-related genes STAYGREEN 1 (SlSGR1) and Senescence-Associated Gene 12 (SlSAG12). In addition, SlWRKY6 
interacted with kinase SlMAPK4 and was phosphorylated at Ser33. Dual-luciferase transient expression assays and electrophoretic 
mobility shift assays indicated that SlWRKY6 persulfidation attenuated its transcriptional regulation of target genes SlSGR1 and 
SlSAG12, whereas SlWRKY6 phosphorylation by SlMAPK4 activated the transcription of target genes to promote fruit ripening. 
Moreover, we provided evidence that SlWRKY6 persulfidation attenuated its SlMAPK4-mediated phosphorylation to inhibit tomato 
fruit ripening. By transient expression of SlWRKY6, SlWRKY6
C396A, SlWRKY6S33A, and SlWRKY6S33D in slwrky6 fruits, we found that 
SlWRKY6 persulfidation attenuated the expression of SlSGR1 and SlSAG12 thereby delaying tomato fruit ripening, while SlWRKY6 
phosphorylation increased the expression of target genes. As tomato fruits ripened, endogenous H 2S production decreased, while 
SlMAPK4 expression increased. Therefore, our findings reveal a model in which SlWRKY6 persulfidation due to higher endogenous H2S 
levels in un-ripened fruit inhibits its ability to activate SlSGR1 and SlSAG12 expression, while SlWRKY6 phosphorylation by SlMAPK4 
activates its transcriptional activity, thereby promoting tomato fruit ripening.

Introduction
Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) is a fruit and vegetable crop rich in 
nutrients that is widely grown in the world. Due to its clear genetic 
background and short growth cycle, it is often used as a model 
plant for studying the mechanism of fruit ripening and senes
-
cence (Wu et al. 2020). Fruit ripening is a complex developmental 
process involving a variety of physiological and biochemical reac-
tions such as changes in fruit color, texture, and accumulation of 
soluble sugars, carotenoids, and volatile organic compounds (
Klee 
and Giovannoni 2011; Giovannoni et al. 2017). The study of fruit 
ripening and senescence is crucial to reveal the mechanism of 
fruit development, which can help improve yield, optimize qual
-
ity, and improve postharvest storage of tomatoes.
Transcription factors (TFs) play essential roles during plant 
growth and development by regulating the transcription of 
downstream genes. In recent years, a plethora of TFs have been 
reported to play important regulatory roles in tomato fruit ripen
-
ing. For instance, MADS-box family protein RIPENING INHIBITOR, 
NON-RIPENING, and COLORLESS NON-RIPENING have been iden-
tified as positive regulators of fruit ripening (Karlova et al. 2014). In 
addition, WRKY TFs are an important family of TF that partici -
pates in a variety of biotic, abiotic, or physiological responses 
(Eulgem and Somssich 2007). WRKY TFs are zinc finger-type pro-
teins containing WRKYGQK heptapeptide at the N-terminus and 
zinc finger structure at the C-terminus (C
4-5CX22-23HXH or 
CX7CX23HXC) and specifically recognize cis-acting element called 
the W-box (TTGACT/C) in the promoters of target genes (Rushton 
et al. 2010). Accumulating studies have shown that WRKY TFs are 
involved in plant growth and development. For instance, wheat 
(Triticum 
aestivum) TaWRKY51 promotes lateral root formation 
by binding to the W-box in the promoter and blocking the expres-
sion of ACS genes involved in ethylene biosynthesis ( Hu et al. 
2018). MdWRKY17 can directly promote the expression of the sal-
icylic acid degradation gene Downy Mildew Resistant 6 (MdDMR6
), 
resulting in reduced resistance to Colletotrichum fructicola (Shan 
et al. 2021). SlWRKY35 could directly activate the expression of 
SlDXS1 leading to the accumulation of carotenoids and thus pro-
moting tomato fruit ripening (Yuan et al. 2022).
Hydrogen sulfide (H 2S) is a gaseous signaling molecule that 
can effectively regulate growth and developmental processes 
such as seed germination, fruit ripening, senescence, and bi
-
otic and abiotic stress responses in plants ( Zhang et al. 2008; 
Hu et al. 2020). H 2S could substantially inhibit the ripening 
and senescence of postharvest fruits and prolong their storage 
time as found in strawberry, tomato, and pear fruits (Hu et al. 
2012; Ziogas et al. 2018; Yao et al. 2020 ). The mechanism of 
H2S in delaying fruit ripening and senescence may involve 
the regulation of redox balance, inhibiting ethylene biosynthe -
sis and antagonizing ethylene effect and signaling pathway 
and energy metabolism in fruits and the integrity of cell wall

and cell membrane (Jia et al. 2018; Hu et al. 2020; Yao et al.

As a signaling molecule, H2S is believed to accomplish its func-
tion in vivo through protein persulfidation (Filipovic et al. 2018). As 
a recently discovered type of post-translational modification of 
proteins, protein S-sulfhydration modification, also known as per-
sulfidation, refers to the transformation of a sulfhydryl group 
(–SH) on a cysteine (Cys) residue of a protein into a persulfidated 
state (–SSH). Aroca et al. (2017)identified 2015 proteins with sulf-
hydryl modifications in Arabidopsis thaliana
, which are involved in 
multiple processes of plant growth and development, suggesting 
that protein sulfhydryl modifications may be a widespread form 
of post-translational modification of proteins. Recent studies 
have found that the autophagy-related protein ATG18 can under
-
go persulfidation at the Cys103 site in response to H2S and thus in-
hibit the autophagy process in A. thaliana under nutrient 
deprivation (Aroca et al. 2021). During H2S-induced stomatal clo-
sure, SnRK2.6 can respond to H2S signal and undergo persulfida-
tion at the Cys131 and Cys137 sites, leading to an increase in the 
activity of the SnRK2.6 kinase and facilitating its binding to the 
ABA-responsive element-binding factor ABF2, which in turn indu
-
ces stomatal closure (Chen et al. 2021). Recently, H2S was found to 
persulfidate E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase 3 BRG3 and reduce its 
ubiquitination activity, which in turn led to subsequently delayed 
ripening of tomatoes (Sun et al. 2023).
In plants, endogenous H2S is produced mainly during cysteine 
catabolism. The cysteine desulfhydrases LCD (L-cysteine desulf-
hydrase) or DCD (D-cysteine desulfhydrase) use L-Cys and D-Cys, 
respectively, as substrates to produce H 2S (Alvarez et al. 2010; 
Scuffi et al. 2014; Zhao et al. 2023). Previously, we reported the 
function of SlLCD1 and SlDCD2 as negative regulators of fruit rip-
ening, further confirming the role of endogenous H2S in delaying 
ripening and senescence ( Hu et al. 2020; Zhao et al. 2023). 
However, whether differential endogenous H2S levels could cause 
differential levels of protein persulfidation still needs further 
research.
In addition to protein persulfidation, phosphorylation has long 
been considered as an important way for regulating protein func-
tions (Zhang et al. 2023). When cells sense signals through receptors, 
the signals are transmitted down the MAPKKK–MAPKK–MAPK cas-
cade through phosphorylation, and the activated MAPKs regulate 
protein functions by phosphorylating downstream TFs, protein kin
-
ases, and other substrates to achieve signal transduction (Danquah 
et al. 2014; Wei et al. 2023). Recently, phosphorylated TFs modified by 
MAPK kinase have been extensively studied in plants. For instance, 
banana MabZIP21 (leucine zipper 21) phosphorylation mediated by 
MaMPK6-3 could increase the expression of softening-related genes, 
thereby promoting fruit ripening in banana (Wu et al. 2022
). 
Phosphorylation of SlBBX17 by SlMPK1 and SlMPK2 positively regu-
lated cold tolerance in tomato (Song et al. 2023). Wei et al. (2023)
showed that an ethylene-induced increase in MdMAPK3 kinase ac-
tivity promoted the phosphorylation of MdNAC72 by MdMAPK3 
and thus apple fruit softening. However, whether and how persulfi
-
dation interferes with phosphorylation in a single protein is still lack 
of evidence.
In the present study, differential persulfidated proteins in SlLCD1 
overexpression leaves were analyzed by 4D-label-free LC/MS 
proteomics and it was noticed that a peptide in a WKRY protein 
(A0A3Q7F586) which was later named as SlWRKY6 showed higher 
intensity of persulfidation. By CRISPR/Cas9 and gene overexpres
-
sion, we showed that SlWRKY6 was a positive regulator of leave 
senescence and tomato fruit ripening by activating STAYGREEN 1 
(
SlSGR1) and Senescence-Associated Gene 12 (SlSAG12) transcription. 
Further evidence indicated that persulfidation of SlWRKY6 by H2S 
attenuated its transcriptional activity to target genes SlSGR1 and 
SlSAG12. However, SlWRKY6 phosphorylation by SlMAPK4 could 
activate the expression of SlSGR1 and SlSAG12 to promote tomato 
fruit ripening. Moreover, we provided evidence that SlWRKY6 
persulfidation mediated by H
2S attenuated SlMAPK4-mediated 
phosphorylation of SlWRKY6 to inhibit tomato fruit ripening. 
Therefore, the current study provides a solid theoretical basis into 
the mechanism of persulfidation and phosphorylation of a single 
protein SlWRKY6 in regulating tomato fruit ripening.
Results
Identification of persulfidation modification 
at Cys396 of SlWRKY6 protein
To explore the role of H2S in regulating tomato fruit ripening, green 
ripening stage tomato fruits were fumigated with H2S. As shown in 
Supplementary Fig. S1, H2S substantially delayed tomato fruit rip-
ening, whether H2S functioned through its mediation of target pro-
tein persulfidation is still unclear. Previously, we created a SlLCD1 
overexpression tomato line which encoding an H2S generating en-
zyme LCD catalyzing L-cysteine to give rise H2S, ammonia, and pyr-
uvate (Hu et al. 2021). SlLCD1 overexpression was found to delay 
dark-induced senescence and fruit ripening. Therefore, we carried 
out 4D-label-free LC/MS proteomics to investigate potential differ-
entially persulfidated proteins in SlLCD1 overexpression and 
wild-type (WT) leaves. Among the 119 peptides that underwent per-
sulfidation, it was noticed that a peptide in a WKRY protein 
(A0A3Q7F586, encoding by Solyc02g080890.2) showed a higher in-
tensity of persulfidation. By homologous comparison, the WKRY 
protein showed high sequence similarity to WRKY6 in A. thaliana 
and therefore named SlWRKY6 in tomato. To investigate whether 
the SlWRKY6 protein could undergo persulfidation in response to 
H
2S, in vitro persulfidation reaction was performed using H2S donor 
NaHS as the sulfhydryl agent, followed by detection by modified 
biotin-switch method. As shown in 
Fig. 1A, NaHS treatment of 
purified HIS–SlWRKY6 protein induced the occurrence of persulfi-
dation and the persulfidation signal increased as NaHS concentra-
tion increased. When dithiothreitol (DTT) was added, the signal of 
persulfidation was eliminated. The result indicated that the re
-
combinant HIS–SlWRKY6 protein expressed in Escherichia coli could 
be sulfhydryl modified by H2S and the sulfhydryl group could be re-
duced by DTT reductant. To determine the amino acid sites in 
SlWRKY6 that were persulfidated, purified HIS–SlWRKY6 protein 
was treated with exogenous H
2S and then analyzed by LC-MS/MS. 
The data revealed that the Cys site at Position 396 of SlWRKY6 
protein could undergo persulfidation (Fig. 1B and Supplementary 
Data Set 1).
To further verify the persulfidated site, we mutated Cys396 to 
Ala in SlWRKY6 (Fig. 1C) and compared the effect of H 2S on the 
modification of SlWRKY6. It was found when Cys396 was mutated 
to Ala, persulfidation signal of SlWRKY6
C396A mutant protein was 
almost fully abolished shown by Western blotting results (Fig. 1D). 
These results suggested that SlWRKY6 could be modified by H
2S 
and the key site of persulfidation happened on Cys396.

SlMAPK4 phosphorylates SlWRKY6 at Ser33
To detect whether SlMAPK4 can phosphorylate SlWRKY6, we pu-
rified recombinant HIS-tagged SlWRKY6 and GST-tagged 
SlMAPK4 to perform in vitro kinase assays by Phos-tag mobility 
shift assay. The protein combination was incubated with lambda 
protein phosphatase (λ
PP) to eliminate the phosphorylation of 
SlWRKY6. As shown in Fig. 4E, the immunoblot analysis with 
anti-HIS antibody showed that SlMAPK4 caused an upward shift 
of the target band of SlWRKY6 while the shift was eliminated by 
λPP 
treatment, indicating that SlMAPK4 were able to phosphory-
late SlWRKY6 in vitro.

Post-translational modifications are essential for protein func-
tion. Accumulating evidence show that many WRKY family pro-
teins can undergo phosphorylation as substrates of MAPKs in 
Arabidopsis (Guan et al. 2014), rice (Wang et al. 2023), and cotton

Arabidopsis (

have demonstrated that the MAPK cascade is involved in the eth-
ylene signaling pathway. For instance, Arabidopsis MAPK6 can in-
crease ethylene biosynthesis by phosphorylating ACS2/ACS6 to 
enhance its protein stability and activity (

Ethylene was found to induce enhancement of MdMAPK3 phos -

(Wei et al. 2023). MAPK3 and MAPK6 can regulate the expression of 
ACS2 and ACS6 genes by phosphorylating the TF WRKY33 (WRKY 
DNA-binding protein 33) in Arabidopsis (Li et al. 2012). Although 
phosphorylation is known to be essential for the ethylene synthe-
sis pathway in fruit ripening by promoting the stability of TFs, 
there are fewer reports on the effects of TF phosphorylation on 
the chlorophyll degradation pathway. In the present work, 
SlMAPK4 is found to interact with SlWRKY6 and to phosphory

SlSAG12 (Fig. 5, F and G). This result is also verified by EMSA as 
when SlWRKY6 is phosphorylated by SlMAPK4, the binding affin-
ity of phosphorylated SlWRKY6 for the target gene promoters is 
clearly enhanced due to the stronger intensity of the bands 
(Fig. 6, D and G). Also, how SlWRKY6 phosphorylation regulates 
tomato fruit ripening is further investigated by transient expres-

SlMAPK4 acts as a molecular bridge linking chlorophyll degrada-

during tomato fruit ripening. Moreover, SlMAPK4 is important 
for the phosphorylation of SlWRKY6, and phosphorylation of 
SlWRKY6 by SlMAPK4 activates the expression of SlWRKY6 
downstream target genes, thereby promoting the ripening of 
SlWRKY6 tomato fruits.

phosphorylation are also explored. When the combination of

insights into the mechanism of persulfidation and phosphoryla

improve the understanding of the molecular basis by which H

inhibits fruit ripening.
Materials and methods
Plant materials and growth conditions
The WT tomato (S. lycopersicum “Micro-Tom”) used in this study 
and transgenic lines constructed in this background (2 CRISPR– 
Cas, 9 mutant lines slwrky6#4 and slwrky6#11, and 2 pBI121- 
FLAG lines SlWRKY6#1 and SlWRKY6#2) were grown in a nutrient 
soil: vermiculite (3:1, v/v) in pots of 10 cm diameter with 16-h 
light/8-h dark cycles at 25°C.
H2S fumigation of tomato fruits
The WT tomato fruits at the green ripening stage were fumigated 
in a 10 L closed container with 100 mL 500 μM NaHS as H
2S donor 
or water at 25°C. Random 10 tomato fruits with similar sizes and 
shapes were selected and each group contained 3 replicates. The 
NaHS solution or water was changed every 24 h and photographs 
were taken daily for phenotypic observation.
Plasmid construction and plant transformation
To construct SlWRKY6 loss-of-function mutant plants, 2 gRNA 
sequences of SlWRKY6 were designed by the CRISPRdirect 
website (http://crispr.dbcls.jp/) (Supplementary Table S1 ). The 
synthesized gRNAs were annealed and integrated with AtU3d 
and AtU3b promoter which were further inserted into the 
pYLCRISPR/Cas9-DN binary vector. To overexpress SlWRKY6
, 
the coding sequence (CDS) of SlWRKY6 without the stop codon 
were amplified from the cDNA of the WT tomato and inserted 
into the pBI121-FLAG vector at the sites of XbaI and XhoI. The 
recombinant plasmids were transformed into Agrobacterium

tomato cv. Micro-Tom by Agrobacterium-mediated cotyledon tissue

confirmed by RT-qPCR and Western blotting with anti-FLAG anti-

used for further study.
Identification of persulfidated proteins  
by LC-MS/MS
The persulfidation proteins were identified according to the meth-
od of Aroca et al. (2017). Tomato leaves of WT and SlLCD1 were 
lysed in the lysis solution of 4% (w/v) SDS and 100 mM Tris/HCl 
at pH 7.6 to extract proteins, followed by protein quantification 
by bicinchoninic acid method. An appropriate amount of protein 
from each sample was trypsinized by filter-aided proteome prep
-
aration, and the peptides were desalted by C18 Cartridge, lyophi-
lized in 40 μ
L of 0.1% (v/v) formic acid solution, and then 
quantified by peptide quantification (OD280). The peptides were 
analyzed by liquid chromatography and MS. MS and MS/MS 
data obtained for individual samples were processed using 
Analyst TF 1.5.1 Software (AB SCIEX).

His–SlWRKY6 were expressed in E. coli Rosetta (DE3) by

Transcriptome analysis
Three biological replicates of mature leaf samples of WT and 
SlWRKY6 overexpression plants grown for 60 d were collected 
for RNA-seq analysis. RNA-seq was performed at OE Biotech 
(China) as described by Wu et al. (2020). The clean reads were 
aligned to the Tomato Genome version SL4.0. The DEGs between 
WT and SlWRKY6 overexpression leaves were analyzed under 
fold change ≥2 and false discovery rate <0.05. KEGG pathway of 
DEGs was analyzed using (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/).
Measurement of chlorophyll and carotenoid in 
tomato leaves and fruits
The contents of chlorophyll and carotenoid were determined by 
the method of Yao et al. (2020). Fresh tomato sample of 2.0 ± 
0.01 g was ground in liquid nitrogen and extracted with 95% (v/v) 
ethanol. The absorbance was measured at 665, 649, and 470 nm. 
Three biological replicates were performed for each sample, and 
each biological replicate includes 3 technical replicates. The results 
were expressed as mg/g FW (fresh weight).
RNA extraction and reverse transcription 
quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) analysis
Total RNA was extracted using FastPure Universal Plant Total 
RNA Isolation Kit (Vazyme, China). Then, cDNA synthesis was 
performed according to Evo M-MLV One-step RT-PCR Kit (AG, 
China). RT-qPCR was performed by the manufacturer’s instruc
-
tions of Light Cycler 96 SYBR GREEN I Master (Roche, USA). The 
relative expression level of genes was analyzed using the 2 −ΔΔCt 
analysis method by normalizing to the reference gene SlTubulin 
(Bustin et al. 2009). Each sample represents 3 biological replicates, 
and each biological replicate includes 3 technical replicates. The 
primers are listed in Supplementary Table S5.
Determination of endogenous H2S production in 
tomato fruit
The production of endogenous H 2S of fruit sample was deter -
mined according to the method of Riemenschneider et al. (2005). 
Fruit samples (1 g) of 35, 40, and 45 DPA were ground in liquid ni-
trogen and homogenized by adding 5 mL of phosphate-buffered 
salt (pH 7.4) solution containing 10 mM L-cysteine and 10 mM pyr-
idoxal 5
′-phosphate and then centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 10 min. 
H2S released from the supernatant was absorbed by zinc acetate 
and then reacted with N,N-dimethyl-phenylenediamine and 
FeCl3 to form methylene blue, then the absorbance was deter -
mined at 670 nm. The experiment was performed in 3 technical 
replicates and repeated for 3 biological replicates.

transformants. Positive interactions were detected on medium

C396A,

and yeast 2-hybrid were carried out between SlWRKY6 trunca -
tions, SlWRKY6 site mutations and SlMAPK4. The primer sequen-

Pull-down assays
To further explore the interaction between SlWRKY6 and 
SlMAPK4, CDSs of SlMAPK4 and SlWRKY6 were cloned into pGEX-

SlWRKY6, respectively. Recombinant GST–SlMAPK4 and 
HIS–SlWRKY6 were expressed from E. coli Rosetta (DE3) by adding 
0.5 mM IPTG overnight at 16°C for 16 h. The pull-down analyses 
were performed referring to the method of Miernyk and Thelen 
(2008) . The protein blots were analyzed using an anti-GST antibody 
and anti-HIS antibody (working dilution 1:5,000) from Beyotime 
(China). The primer sequences are listed in Supplementary 
Table S3.
FLC assay
According to Yao’s et al. (2023) method for FLC assay, SlWRKY6 
was labeled to the N-terminal end of LUC to form NLuc- 
SlWRKY6, while SlMAPK4 was labeled with the C-terminal end 
of LUC to form CLuc-SlMAPK4. NLuc-SlWRKY6 and CLuc- 
SlMAPK4 constructs were then transferred into Agrobacterium sep-
arately, mixed at a ratio of 1:1 (v/v) and co-infiltrated into Nicotiana

transferred to light incubation for 2 to 3 d, the firefly luciferase ac-
tivity was measured by Enhanced Firefly Luciferase Reporter Gene 
Assay Kit (Beyotime, China). The primer sequences are listed in 
Supplementary Table S7.
BiFC assay
The BiFC assay was carried out based on the method of Song et al. 
(2023). Recombinant vectors SlWRKY6-cYFP and SlMAPK4-nYFP 
were transfected into Agrobacterium strain GV3101 separately, 
and transient expressions were performed in N. benthamiana 
leaves of 4 wk growth. A laser confocal scanning microscope 
(Nikon, Japan) with an excitation wavelength at 511 nm and emis-
sion wavelength of 525 nm under laser intensity of 20% were used 
to observe the fluorescence signal in leaf cells after 2 d of infiltra
-
tion. The primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table S7.

Statistical analysis
All experiments use at least 3 biological replicates. One-way 
ANOVA was analyzed using SPSS statistical software version 
24.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Three independent experiments 
(replications) were used to calculate the mean and standard 
deviation (SD). Significant differences among the parameters 
means (P <0.05) were analyzed by Student’s t-test or Duncan’s 
multiple tests. The date processing and graphics rendering were 
plotted by Origin Pro 9.0.
